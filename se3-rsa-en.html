SE3 · RSA mini-project

What this is: the individual project of the course — 10 % of the grade. You build a toy RSA, actually use it to exchange messages with the rest of the class, explain why it works using your numbers, and find out how far it holds before it breaks.

What is assessed: understanding, not code. The calls fit in ten lines and anyone can write them; what gets marked is whether you know where each number comes from and which step rests on what.

Learning outcome RA7 — use of mathematical software. And along the way RA1–RA2: the modular arithmetic of Module 1, applied.
Weight 10 % of the final grade
Estimated time 7–12 h in total, spread over the semester (per-phase breakdown in the timeline). This is not a weekend job: phase 3 depends on other people replying.
Submission A notebook (Colab or .ipynb), through the virtual classroom

Before you start

Arrive with exercise 4 of Session 4 already done: the full scheme set up with 6–8 digit primes, encrypting and decrypting. It is the warm-up for this, and it saves you fighting the code while you are trying to think about the mathematics.

The starting point is the Python block in the notes, §4.3 “The demo in Python”, which you can run and edit on the page itself.

Phase 1 · Your keys

Generate your primes p≠qp \neq q, your n=pqn = pq and your φ=(p−1)(q−1)\varphi = (p-1)(q-1). Use primes of 6 to 8 digits: large enough that the exchange is not trivial, small enough that everything runs instantly.

Choose your ee, and be ready to justify it. Do not copy the 65537 from the box just because it is there: exercise 9 of the session gives you the tools to decide it yourself. If you end up choosing 65537, fine — but you will have to say why.

Compute dd and check that it decrypts.

The condition p≠qp \neq q is not a precaution: it is a hypothesis of Theorem 1.4.5. If you make them equal, (p−1)(q−1)(p-1)(q-1) stops being the number that makes decryption work.

Phase 2 · The public directory

Publish your (n,e)(n, e) — only that — in the “Public key directory” thread of the forum, in a message with your name on it.

A public key belongs in public: that thread is not a metaphor for a key directory, it is a key directory. Your dd, your pp and your qq never leave your machine.

Its own deadline. This phase takes fifteen minutes and has a deadline of its own, well before the final submission: if the directory is not complete in time, whoever publishes last is left with nobody to exchange with.

Phase 3 · The exchange

Four roles. All four are required, and all four leave a trace in the forum.

1 · Sender. Pick someone from the directory, encrypt a numeric message with their public key and post it addressed to them.

2 · Receiver. Decrypt the message sent to you.

3 · Signer. Reply with the decrypted message and its signature s=mdmodns = m^d \bmod n, computed with your private key.

4 · Verifier. Pick the signed reply of a third person — not yours, not the one who wrote to you — and verify their signature with their public key. Post whether it checks out.

Why the signature. Posting the decrypted message proves nothing: whoever encrypted it already knew it. The signature does, because it can only be produced with dd, and anyone can check it with the public key. It is the decryption theorem with the roles swapped.

Nobody to exchange with? My key is in the directory like anyone else’s. If you are left out, you exchange with me and carry on. No need to warn anyone or wait to be assigned a partner.

Phase 4 · The report

Four parts. All of it with your numbers, not with letters.

(a) Why it works.

  • Where your dd comes from: the steps of the extended Euclidean algorithm, not just the result.
  • Check e⋅d≡1(modφ)e \cdot d \equiv 1 \pmod{\varphi} and write the concrete kk in ed=1+kφed = 1 + k\varphi.
  • Why med≡m(modn)m^{ed} \equiv m \pmod n: Fermat modulo pp, Fermat modulo qq, and the Chinese remainder theorem to glue the two congruences. With your pp and your qq.

What counts as “with your numbers” — this is where the marks are. It is not taking Theorem 1.4.5 and checking in Python that with your pp and qq the message comes back — that verifies one case, and you already did it in phase 1. It is redoing the whole argument instantiated: writing your kk, applying Fermat to each prime separately, and gluing the two congruences with the CRT. There is a full sample below.

(b) Why YOUR ee. What you ruled out and why. And why not e=3e = 3, which would be cheaper.

(c) Break it on purpose. Pick one of the two and show it running:

  • Take an ee with gcd(e,φ)≠1\mathrm{gcd}(e, \varphi) \neq 1. Which step fails, exactly?
  • Or take p=qp = q. Where does the proof collapse?

Saying that it stops working is not enough: you have to point at the place where the hypothesis was doing its job.

(d) What it lacks to be real. Your ladder from phase 5 says something about key size: say it. And explain why your RSA, correct as it is, still would not do for anything serious.

Phase 5 · The attack ladder

You have a ladder of targets — pairs (n,e)(n, e) with an encrypted message, of increasing size — in SE3 · Ladder of targets. None of them belongs to a classmate: they are mine, put there for you to break.

For each one you can: factor nn, recover φ\varphi, recover dd, and read the message.

What you hand in is not “I broke three”, it is the curve: how far you got, how long the last one to fall took, and where your machine stalled. And then one question: if that is your wall, and real RSA uses keys of some 600 digits, how much margin is there?

Still hungry? There are bigger targets, with no guarantee they will fall, among the optional grade-raising challenges — challenge R6.

The sample: what level of detail is expected

Part (a) is worth 3 of the 10 points, so here is exactly what is being asked for, worked out with toy primes. Do not use these: they are absurdly small and are here only to show the shape. With your 6–8 digit primes the text is the same; the numbers change.

Sample — part (a) with p=11p = 11, q=13q = 13

My keys: n=11⋅13=143n = 11 \cdot 13 = 143 and φ=10⋅12=120\varphi = 10 \cdot 12 = 120. I take e=7e = 7, which is prime and does not divide 120, so gcd(7,120)=1\mathrm{gcd}(7,120) = 1.

Where my dd comes from. Extended Euclid on 120=17⋅7+1120 = 17\cdot 7 + 1, so 1=120−17⋅71 = 120 - 17 \cdot 7, and reading modulo 120: −17⋅7≡1-17 \cdot 7 \equiv 1. Since −17≡103(mod120)-17 \equiv 103 \pmod{120}, my d=103d = 103.

The check, with kk made explicit. e⋅d=7⋅103=721=1+6⋅120e \cdot d = 7 \cdot 103 = 721 = 1 + 6 \cdot 120, that is ed=1+kφed = 1 + k\varphi with 𝐤=𝟔\;\mathbf{k = 6}.

Why med≡m(mod143)m^{ed} \equiv m \pmod{143}, for every mm. I write med=m1+6⋅120=m⋅(m120)6m^{ed} = m^{1 + 6 \cdot 120} = m \cdot \left(m^{120}\right)^{6}, and look at it modulo each prime separately.

Modulo 11. If 11∤m11 \nmid m, Fermat gives m10≡1m^{10} \equiv 1. And 120120 is a multiple of 1010 — precisely because φ\varphi carries the factor p−1=10p-1 = 10 inside it —: 120=10⋅12120 = 10 \cdot 12, so m120=(m10)12≡1m^{120} = (m^{10})^{12} \equiv 1 and therefore med≡m⋅1=mm^{ed} \equiv m \cdot 1 = m. If 11∣m11 \mid m, both sides are ≡0\equiv 0, and the congruence holds too.

Modulo 13. The same, with q−1=12q - 1 = 12: if 13∤m13 \nmid m, then m12≡1m^{12} \equiv 1 and 120=12⋅10120 = 12 \cdot 10, so m120≡1m^{120} \equiv 1 and med≡mm^{ed} \equiv m. And if 13∣m13 \mid m, both sides are 00.

Gluing the two with the CRT. I have 11∣(med−m)11 \mid (m^{ed} - m) and 13∣(med−m)13 \mid (m^{ed} - m). Since 11 and 13 are coprime, their product divides the difference: 143∣(med−m)143 \mid (m^{ed} - m), that is med≡m(mod143)m^{ed} \equiv m \pmod{143}. ◼\blacksquare

One case, to see it run. m=7m = 7: I encrypt c=77mod143=6c = 7^{7} \bmod 143 = 6, I decrypt 6103mod143=76^{103} \bmod 143 = 7 ✓. And m=11m = 11, which is a multiple of pp and therefore not coprime with nn: 117⋅103≡1111^{7\cdot103} \equiv 11 all the same ✓ — the case that the Fermat + CRT proof covers and Euler’s theorem, on its own, does not.

What makes this worth the 3 points, rather than the three lines of checking it in Python, are the three things in bold: the kk written out, the reason φ\varphi is a multiple of p−1p-1 and of q−1q-1 (which is why it works, not an accident of these numbers), and the CRT step done, not cited. Parts (b), (c) and (d) ask for the same level.

On the use of AI

Allowed and recommended, declaring what you used and what for, as the course guide on AI use sets out.

And you may as well know how this is built: your numbers are yours, so no generic report will do; the forum thread is public and timestamped, so phase 3 is yours to do; the counterfactual requires causing a failure and diagnosing it, not describing that one exists; and the ladder is a measurement on your machine. You can use AI all the way through — what is marked is whether you understand the result.

What this project is meant to leave you with

That RSA is not a four-line recipe that works by magic. Every line rests on a theorem you have proved this module: Euclid gives you dd, Fermat and the Chinese remainder theorem give the message back, and the difficulty of factoring is the only thing separating your key from anyone else’s.

And that this “only thing” is measurable. By the end of phase 5 you will know, with data from your own laptop, exactly what your toy is worth — and why the real one needs to be so big.
